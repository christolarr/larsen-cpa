SB 1103 and CAM Charges for Small California Tenants

Since January 1, 2025, California Civil Code Section 1950.9, added by SB 1103, limits how a landlord can charge certain small commercial tenants for building operating costs, including common area maintenance (CAM). This page summarizes what the statute requires and how a CAM review fits in.

Who It Covers

The statute protects "qualified commercial tenants": microenterprises as defined in Business and Professions Code Section 18000, restaurants with fewer than 10 employees, and nonprofit organizations with fewer than 20 employees. To be treated as one, the tenant gives the landlord written notice that it is a qualified commercial tenant, together with a self-attestation of its number of employees.

Section 1950.9 applies to leases executed, and tenancies commenced or renewed, on or after January 1, 2025. It also reaches earlier leases that contain no provision about building operating costs. Whether a particular lease is covered is a legal question for an attorney.

What the Landlord Must Do Before Charging Operating Costs

  • Allocate the costs proportionately per tenant, by square footage or another method, substantiated through supporting documentation.
  • Before the lease is signed, give the tenant notice that it may inspect supporting documentation of building operating costs on written request.
  • Within 30 days of a written request, provide supporting documentation of the costs previously incurred or reasonably expected.
  • Leave out expenses the tenant pays directly to a third party.
  • Not change the method used to allocate costs during the lease in a way that increases the tenant's share, unless the tenant receives written notice and supporting documentation for the change.

These requirements cannot be waived in the lease, and a landlord that violates them may be liable for damages.

How a CAM Review Fits In

A qualified commercial tenant can ask the landlord in writing for the supporting documentation behind its operating cost charges. A CAM and lease compliance review then compares the annual reconciliation against the lease and the documentation provided, and records each charge the lease or the documentation does not support. Findings are delivered in writing with the lease provision and a supporting schedule.

What to Send

  • The lease and all amendments.
  • The most recent CAM or operating expense reconciliation.
  • Any supporting documentation the landlord has provided.
